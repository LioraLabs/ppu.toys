# Mirror (showcase: HDMA)

A lake at dusk: a big striped sun, pagoda, pines, a fishing boat, rising sky
lanterns and stars. The whole lower 92 lines are not drawn. Every water
scanline is an `hdma()` write that points BG1–3's vertical scroll back up at
the row it mirrors (`scroll.y = r - y`, `r = 2·horizon − 1 − y`), shifts it
sideways for perspective-spaced ripples, and darkens it with fixed-colour
subtraction (green pulled hardest, so gold reflects rose).

Tricks used:
- **Per-line VOFS/HOFS** on three layers: the mirror, ripples, a gust that
  rolls toward the viewer, small vertical jitter that tears the reflection
  into strips, heat shimmer above the far range.
- **Per-line CGRAM**: backdrop sky gradient (breathing between two
  gradients over the loop), sun colour ramp, sun stripe gaps (the sun's index
  takes the sky colour on gap lines), haze on the far range, lit clouds.
  Indices are looked up by *source row*, so the reflection gets them free.
- **Colour math**: fixed subtract on water lines, fixed add for a 1-line gold
  seam at the horizon.
- **Parallax** camera sway (BG1 ±16, BG2 ±8, BG3 ±2 px).
- **Sprites** can't be mirrored by scroll, so each lantern has an explicit
  `flip_y` twin placed at the mirrored height and ripple offset.

Art is generated by `build_art.py` as palette *indices* (index k stored as
`rgb(8k,0,0)`, which the importer sorts into slot k); `main.lua` owns every
real colour. 12-second seamless loop (`ppu check --loop 12` passes); the
ripples calm to a near-perfect mirror at the loop seam (t≈0).

Best 6-s window for recording: **t = 0–6** — starts glassy-calm (the
mirror reads as a mirror), then the wind picks up and the gust rolls in.
