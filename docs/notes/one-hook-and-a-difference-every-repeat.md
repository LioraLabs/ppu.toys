title: A song has one hook reused in new clothes, and every repeat earns one audible difference
codes: []
---
A game tune holds together around one hook: a short cell a player could hum, coming back through the
piece with its setting changed, an octave down, under a new bass line, with the echo voice added
([[a-written-echo-trails-the-lead-by-an-eighth]]). The tune itself is yours; a technique you borrow
from a reference piece travels, its melody does not.

A section played twice the same is a copy. The second time earns one difference a listener hears: the
echo comes in, the harmony moves up, the last bar climbs into what follows. Read the arrangement order
against the patterns to catch the copies.

A key lift, the last chorus a tone higher, barely moves the loudness by itself. Pair it with a texture or
level step (another voice, the drums doubling) so it lands as a lift
([[sections-step-in-loudness-terraces]]). Phrases that always end on the barline sound mechanical: let a
note ring across it, group eighths 3+3+2, and breathe where the drums already turn.
