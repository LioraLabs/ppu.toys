title: Revise a song under timestamped notes with the timeline frozen, and prove what won is unchanged
codes: []
---
A person with ears outranks the check on taste, and gives notes as timestamps. Revise in rounds:

1. **Freeze the timeline.** Between rounds, no pattern changes length and the arrangement order stays
   put; otherwise "0:48" means one passage in the old version and another in the new. Change the
   structure only when a listener asks for that.
2. **Map the timestamp.** The check lists each slot's start time: turn "1:12" into a pattern and a beat
   before changing anything.
3. **Turn the complaint into numbers.** Two parts that clash differ by some interval, share some beats,
   and sit at some level against each other.
   Fix the cause the numbers show: two parts a third apart at equal level is a register problem, solved
   by an octave ([[each-part-has-one-job-and-one-register]]).
4. **Protect what won.** The parts nobody complained about stay identical: read the song before and after
   and compare them.
5. **Check for overshoot.** Measure the fixed thing again. A part turned down until the lead vanished is
   a new fault.

Write a line per round: the changes and their reasons, the parts left alone and the comparison that
shows it, and for each open worry the change you would try next ([[compose-by-measuring-what-you-cannot-hear]]).
