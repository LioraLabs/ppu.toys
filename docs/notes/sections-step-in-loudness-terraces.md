id: sections-step-in-loudness-terraces
kind: concept
title: A song's sections step in loudness terraces, a verse a few dB under its chorus and an opening you can hear
codes: []
---
Design the loudness map the check prints per arrangement slot
([[compose-by-measuring-what-you-cannot-hear]]): a chorus should measure 3 to 5 dB louder than the verse
before it, and a quiet section sits well below either. Sections that all measure within a decibel of each other have no shape, however different
their notes.

Eight voices with fixed row volumes change level in jumps, not swells, so let each section arrive at its
own level instead of crawling up to it. A bar before the peak, pull the kick and bass out: the drop is
what makes the peak feel high when they return. A sustained bass holds a
quiet section up: fewer or shorter bass notes quiet it where a lower `vol` barely does.

The opening is not silence. Around -18 dB rms in the first bar reads as a song starting; far under that
reads as a broken toy. Remeasure the whole map after every change, since raising one section shrinks
the step to its neighbours.
